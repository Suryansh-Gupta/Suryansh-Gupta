# Regulatory, Liability, and Trust Risks for an "Autopilot" AI Accounting Manager (as of Nov 2026)

Scope note: research was capped at about 17 tool calls. Several primary documents (Penrose page body, vendor ToS) could not be fetched; those items are listed under Gaps. Some well-established background law (Loving v. IRS, §6662) is marked "background" and cited to official pages rather than to fetched text.

## 1. What work legally requires a CPA, and what unlicensed software can do (PTIN, Circular 230, state boards, firm ownership changes)

### Takeaway
Bookkeeping, reconciliation, close, and management reporting are unlicensed activities in the US. The CPA monopoly covers attest work (audits, reviews, and in most states compilations issued with a report) and use of the "CPA" title. Tax preparation for pay is not CPA-restricted, but anyone who prepares returns for compensation needs a PTIN and is exposed to preparer penalties. CPA-firm ownership rules (majority CPA ownership in most states) are under live pressure from private equity, but in the sources found, no UAA/NASBA model-rule change had been finalized by late 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Most states require CPAs to hold a majority of a firm's equity and voting rights. At least 44 jurisdictions require non-CPA owners to be active individuals in the business, which restricts passive investors. PE investors usually get in through alternative practice structures (APS): an attest-only CPA firm that is majority CPA-owned, plus a separate non-CPA "services" company. — [NASBA 2025 PE Task Force White Paper (Nov 2025)](https://nasba.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2025-PE-Task-Force-White-Paper_FINAL.pdf)
- State boards find PE-backed multistate firms hard to police for the 51% CPA-ownership rule. Regulators sometimes don't know a firm has new non-CPA owners. — [NYSSCPA Trusted Professional (Sep 2025)](https://nysscpa.org/news/1050135-state-boards-confront-private-equitys-accounting-footprint-2025-09-09); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/legal-ethics/private-equity-deals-leave-state-accounting-boards-on-sidelines)
- The AICPA put revised APS / independence ethics guidance for PE-backed firms out for a 120-day comment period. The APS provisions have been in the Code since 2000. — [AICPA](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/news/article/aicpa-seeks-feedback-on-revised-ethics-guidance-for-alternative-practice-structures); [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/aicpa-revises-ethics-proposals-for-alternative-practice-structures)
- Oregon and Virginia boards are exploring rule and licensing changes. Oregon is targeting the 2026 legislative session. — [CLEAR (Sep 2025)](https://www.clearhq.org/news/private-equity-ownership-of-accounting-firms-poses-challenges-for-state-boards-9-10-25)
- Market example of the split structure: in the Sikich/Basis partnership, Sikich CPA LLC (a licensed CPA firm) does audit and attest, while Sikich LLC and its subsidiaries do tax and advisory. That keeps attest inside a licensed entity. — [Sikich](https://www.sikich.com/insight/sikich-partners-with-basis-to-advance-ai-enabled-accounting-services/)
- PTIN rules: anyone who prepares, or substantially assists in preparing, a federal return for compensation must have a PTIN. Non-signing preparers also need one. — [IRS PTIN FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/node/6322)
- Circular 230 uses the IRC §7701(a)(36) / Treas. Reg. 301.7701-15 definition of "tax return preparer." Furnishing only "typing, reproduction, or other mechanical assistance" is excluded. — [Federal Register, 2011 final regs](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2011/06/03/2011-13666/regulations-governing-practice-before-the-internal-revenue-service); [The Tax Adviser](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2011/may/hodes-may11/)
- Circular 230 §10.34 was harmonized with the §6694(a) "substantial authority" standard. — [Journal of Accountancy (2011)](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/issues/2011/aug/circular230regs/)
- Basis says it "demonstrated the first AI agent to complete an end-to-end 1065 tax return autonomously." It still positions this as "accountants and agents working together" and sells to CPA firms rather than directly to taxpayers. — [CPA Practice Advisor (Feb 2026)](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/02/24/basis-raises-100-million-to-deploy-ai-agents-for-accounting-firms/178759/)
- Background: after *Loving v. IRS* (D.C. Cir. 2014), the IRS cannot mandate competency licensing of unenrolled preparers, so non-credentialed paid preparation is legal with a PTIN. The voluntary Annual Filing Season Program exists. — [IRS AFSP page](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/annual-filing-season-program)

### Inferences
- An autopilot product can legally do bookkeeping, reconciliation, close, and financial-statement preparation without a CPA. It must not issue anything that looks like an attest report, compilation report, or review, or use "CPA" in marketing unless a licensed CPA firm stands behind it.
- Selling tax filing for a fee probably makes the company (and possibly an employee) a "tax return preparer." Software sold as a self-prep tool (TurboTax model) generally is not a preparer, because the user prepares the return. An "autopilot" product that prepares and files on the customer's behalf for a fee looks much more like a preparer than a software tool. That brings in PTIN requirements, §6694/§6695 penalties, Circular 230 exposure for any credentialed signer, and e-file provider (EFIN) obligations. No IRS guidance on AI-agent-as-preparer was found.
- The dominant structure for anything near attest or tax is an APS-style split: a tech/services company plus an affiliated or partner CPA firm that signs.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether the AICPA's revised APS guidance was finalized, whether NASBA amended the UAA, or what Oregon and Virginia enacted in 2026.
- Found no state board ruling or IRS guidance on whether an autonomous AI agent's preparation counts as "mechanical assistance" or as preparer activity.
- Did not verify which states treat compilation without a report as restricted.

## 2. Liability: who bears errors, penalties, insurance, contract terms, incidents (Bench, Botkeeper)

### Takeaway
Under the tax system, the taxpayer carries primary liability for underpayments and penalties regardless of who or what prepared the return. A paid preparer adds its own §6694/§6695 exposure. Commercially, vendors push risk back through low liability caps, "you must review" clauses, and sometimes capped penalty-reimbursement guarantees. AI-specific E&O exclusions are spreading, so an AI-first firm may find its errors uninsured. The biggest observed trust failures in this category so far are operational and vendor-failure events (Bench in Dec 2024, Botkeeper in Feb 2026), not documented AI-error lawsuits.

### Cited Findings
- Preparer penalties: §6694(a) for unreasonable positions is the greater of $1,000 or 50% of income derived. §6694(b) for willful or reckless conduct is the greater of $5,000 or 75%. Amounts came from a study guide, so verify current figures. — [open-exam-prep study guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/cpa-reg/circular-230/preparer-penalties)
- Background: the §6662 accuracy-related penalty (generally 20% of the underpayment for negligence or substantial understatement) falls on the taxpayer. — [IRS accuracy-related penalty page](https://www.irs.gov/payments/accuracy-related-penalty)
- Bench shut down Dec 27, 2024, which locked customers out right before tax season and led it to advise IRS extensions. Employer.com acquired it days later. The active customer base was estimated at about 12,000 US customers versus a claimed 35,000. Customers had to agree to transfer data to Employer.com to regain access and could not easily export and leave. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/30/bench-to-be-acquired-after-abruptly-shutting-down); [TechCrunch on data](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/10/bench-customers-are-now-being-forced-to-hand-over-their-data-or-risk-losing-it-they-say); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/bench-accounting-to-be-acquired-by-employer-com-following-abrupt-shutdown/)
- After the acquisition, customers alleged they were being charged for books and returns they had already paid for, and one filed a lawsuit. The company disputes this. — [TechCrunch (Mar 2025)](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/14/bench-is-charging-people-for-services-they-already-paid-for-some-customers-say)
- Bench raised over $100M and reached more than 10,000 clients. In a "final effort" it cut staff and leaned on automation, including the "BenchGPT" chatbot, before failing. — [Accounting Today (Feb 2025)](https://www.accountingtoday.com/articles/how-a-once-hot-accounting-fintechs-bet-on-ai-led-to-bankruptcy)
- Botkeeper, an AI bookkeeping and outsourcing provider for CPA firms, announced its closure on Feb 7, 2026 after 11 years. The CEO cited consolidation among its largest clients: about 10 firms made up 30 to 40% of revenue. Most clients reportedly had no advance warning. — [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/02/09/botkeeper-is-closing-its-doors/177677/); [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/what-brought-down-botkeeper); [CFO Brew](https://www.cfobrew.com/stories/2026/02/17/botkeeper-what-went-wrong)
- In a vendor-sponsored Jan 2026 survey of Canadian accountants about errors from ChatGPT-style tools, the most common errors were incorrect interpretation of business expenses (44%) and incorrect tax claims (43%). — [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/01/13/are-businesses-losing-money-by-using-chatgpt-style-tax-and-financial-advice/176233/)
- A commercial blog gave an anecdote of an AI bookkeeping tool coding six months of a SaaS subscription to "Office Supplies." Treat it as anecdotal. — [beancount.io (Jul 2026)](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/15/ai-bookkeeping-software-2026-docyt-zeni-rillet-guide)
- Insurance: CPA-firm professional liability insurers are converging on AI as a risk requiring governance. — [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/insurers-eyeing-ai-risk-at-cpa-firms)
- Berkley introduced an "absolute" AI exclusion across D&O, E&O, and fiduciary products. Hamilton and Philadelphia Insurance have generative-AI exclusions. ISO's January 2026 CGL forms exclude AI-related injury. Standalone or affirmative AI coverage is emerging from Armilla, Mayflower, Embroker, Corgi, and Counterpart (Nov 2025). — [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/end-silent-ai-emerging-ai-exclusions-coverage-fragmentation-and-practical-implications); [Independent Agent](https://www.independentagent.com/vu_resource/artificial-intelligence-exclusions-are-creeping-into-insurance-but-cyber-policies-arent-the-issue-yet/); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/insurance/insurer-ai-exclusions-spark-policyholder-alarm-on-coverage-gaps)
- Sources conflict on how widespread exclusions are. One says most US and Canadian professional liability carriers have "said nothing" about AI in their policy wording. — [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/professional-liability/why-your-clients-probably-arent-covered-for-ai-597354.aspx)
- A July 2026 suit, *Craddock v. OpenAI*, alleges ChatGPT generated deficient legal agreements. It is an early test of AI-advice liability, but not an accounting case. — [Daily Journal](https://dailyjournal.com/articles/392879-business-owner-sues-openai-over-chatgpt-s-allegedly-faulty-legal-guidance)

### Inferences
- For an autopilot product, the customer remains legally liable to the IRS and states. The vendor's exposure is contractual (breach, negligence), plus preparer penalties if it prepares returns for pay. Expect contracts to include fee-based liability caps (commonly 12 months of fees in SaaS), disclaimers of tax advice, and customer-review obligations. Those terms undercut the "autopilot" marketing promise, which creates a messaging and regulatory tension (UDAP/FTC "AI washing" risk if the product claims no review is needed).
- Bench and Botkeeper show that vendor continuity and data portability are first-order trust risks. Buyers and their CPAs will ask for escrow, export, and wind-down commitments.
- Without affirmative AI coverage, an AI-first firm's errors may fall into exclusions. The insurance market is fragmenting, so buying affirmative AI E&O is a necessary budget line.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch competitor ToS (Pilot's terms URL returned 404; Digits, Zeni, and Puzzle were not checked). No verified liability-cap amounts or penalty-guarantee terms are recorded here.
- Found no court case or IRS/state enforcement action tied specifically to an AI bookkeeping or tax startup's substantive error, 2025–2026.
- Sales tax and 1099 penalty specifics (e.g., §6721/6722 information-return penalties) were not researched.

## 3. Security, privacy, bank data access, and money movement (SOC 1/2, GLBA, 1033, MTLs)

### Takeaway
Tax preparers, and likely bookkeepers handling consumer financial data, are "financial institutions" under the GLBA / FTC Safeguards Rule and need a written information security plan. SOC 2 Type II is the market-standard security attestation. A SOC 1 (ICFR) report becomes relevant once audited customers rely on the system for financial reporting. The CFPB 1033 open-banking rule is enjoined and being rewritten, and bank data-access fees (JPMorgan) are likely to become legal. Paying bills triggers money-transmission analysis: use the agent-of-payee exemption or a licensed partner, because a sponsor bank does not by itself solve licensing.

### Cited Findings
- The FTC Safeguards Rule (GLBA) applies to businesses meeting the broad "financial institution" definition, explicitly including tax preparers. It requires a WISP with the nine elements in 16 CFR 314.4. — [CPA Practice Advisor (Oct 2026)](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/10/02/what-the-ftc-safeguards-rule-actually-requires-from-tax-and-accounting-firms/190980/); [The Tax Adviser](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2023/may/complying-with-the-safeguards-rule-for-information-security.html)
- The IRS may treat Safeguards Rule violations as violations of Rev. Proc. 2007-40, which governs e-file providers, so EFIN status is at risk. IRS Pub 4557 and Pub 5708 (a WISP template) are the IRS resources. — [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-security-summit-remind-tax-pros-they-need-a-written-information-security-plan-to-protect-client-data); [IRS Tax Security 2.0](https://www.irs.gov/node/70076)
- The AICPA Code confidentiality rule means uploading client data to third-party AI platforms without consent and data-processing agreements may breach confidentiality. This matters for any CPA partner firm using the product. — [Thomson Reuters blog](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/blog/how-to-use-ai-in-audit-without-creating-inspection-risk/)
- CFPB §1033: the first compliance date, April 1, 2026, passed unenforced because a federal court enjoined enforcement during reconsideration. The CFPB issued an ANPR in Aug 2025. In Aug 2026 it sent a "Personal Financial Data Rights Reconsideration" proposal to OIRA. — [Cozen O'Connor (2026)](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/publications/2026/section-1033-compliance-date-open-banking-rule-enjoined-and-under-reconsideration); [Ballard Spahr Consumer Finance Monitor (Aug 6, 2026)](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/08/06/cfpb-sends-new-section-1033-open-banking-proposal-to-oira-for-review/); [MoFo (Aug 2025)](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/250826-on-reconsideration-cfpb-issues-another-anpr)
- The 2024 rule banned data-access fees. The rewrite reportedly would allow banks to charge after a free tier of requests. JPMorgan told aggregators it would charge fintechs, with the highest fees for payments-focused companies. — [PYMNTS (2026)](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/2026/cfpb-readies-changes-to-open-banking-rule/); [CRS IF13117](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13117.html)
- Banks and aggregators are negotiating bilateral access agreements covering liability, security, SLAs, audit rights, and fees. New York open-banking bills (Mar 2026) remain in committee. — [Ballard Spahr (Jun 2026)](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/06/26/open-banking-regulation-in-2026-federal-regulation-resurfaces-as-states-bring-data-sharing-into-focus/)
- Important nuance: §1033 covers *consumer* accounts. Business-account data access for SMB bookkeeping relies on bank and aggregator contracts rather than a statutory right (inference from the rule's consumer scope; see CRS).
- Money transmission: many states exempt an "agent of the payee" under a written contract (e.g., Cal. Fin. Code §2010(l)), and bill pay is a standard use case. Exemptions vary by state. A sponsor bank does not "cover" the fintech's licensing obligations. Pooling user funds in an omnibus FBO account can make the fintech a money transmitter. Unlicensed transmission can be a federal crime (18 USC 1960). — [Taft (licensing roadmaps)](https://www.taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/fastest-route-to-market-money-transmission-licensing-roadmaps-for-fintechs/); [Lithic](https://lithic.com/blog/money-services-business); [CA DFPI exemptions](https://dfpi.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/337/2020/01/MTA-Exemptions.pdf)

### Inferences
- Minimum trust stack for an autopilot product: SOC 2 Type II, a GLBA Safeguards WISP, and an AI data-processing addendum (no training on customer data, subprocessor list including LLM providers). Plan for a SOC 1 Type II once mid-market customers with external audits rely on the product for ICFR. Auditors will ask for it as a service organization.
- Bank-data unit economics are at risk if the 1033 rewrite allows fees and aggregators pass them through. Diversifying across direct bank APIs and multiple aggregators, plus statement ingestion, is a hedge.
- For bill pay, the low-risk path is to orchestrate payments through a licensed partner (e.g., Ramp/Bill.com-style rails or a licensed payments provider) or through the customer's own bank, and avoid holding funds.

### Gaps
- No primary source compared SOC 1 and SOC 2 for this use case. The distinction above reflects standard AICPA SOC definitions and was not freshly cited.
- Did not research state privacy laws (CCPA/CPRA, which exempts GLBA-covered data at the data level), GDPR/UK GDPR specifics, or the status of the FTC's proposed Safeguards Rule amendments.
- Did not find how Ramp, Mercury, or Bill.com structure licensing for their bill pay.

## 4. AI-specific regulation and audit-standard acceptance (EU AI Act, Colorado, PCAOB/AICPA)

### Takeaway
Bookkeeping and close automation is generally not a "high-risk" use under the EU AI Act. Annex III targets areas such as creditworthiness, employment, and essential services, and high-risk deadlines are being pushed back by the Digital Omnibus anyway. Colorado's AI Act was stayed and then repealed and replaced in 2026, so US state AI law exposure is low for back-office accounting unless the product makes consequential decisions such as credit. The auditing bodies have issued guidance rather than standards. Auditors may rely on AI-produced records only with normal evidence testing, and an AI-generated output alone is not sufficient audit evidence.

### Cited Findings
- EU: provisional agreement on the Digital Omnibus on AI (May 7, 2026), adopted by the European Parliament on June 16, 2026 and pending Council approval. It delays Annex III standalone high-risk obligations by 16 months and moves Annex I embedded products to Aug 2028. The aim was to finish before the Aug 2, 2026 high-risk start. — [Sidley Data Matters (Jun 22, 2026)](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/eu-legislators-agree-to-delay-for-highrisk-ai-rules)
- Colorado: SB 24-205 was delayed to June 30, 2026 by SB 25B-004 (Aug 2025). Enforcement was reportedly stayed by a federal magistrate on Apr 27, 2026. SB 26-189 (May 2026) reportedly repealed and replaced the Act, with implementation reportedly moved to Jan 1, 2027. Sources are secondary and partially conflicting. — [Akin Gump tracker](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/colorado-postpones-implementation-of-colorado-ai-act-sb-24-205); [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/colorado-s-ai-reset-two-weeks-a-white-1874953/); [Constangy](https://www.constangy.com/constangy-cyber-advisor/ai-regulation-in-flux-u-s-and-global-shift-toward-lighter-touch); [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/issues/ai-regulation-brussels-blinks-doj-sues-a-state-connecticut)
- AICPA practice aid on AI in financial statement audits (Oct 13, 2026). The AICPA chief auditor said AI is "not changing our professional requirements with respect to professional skepticism, use of judgment, appropriate supervision, and review." Further guidance on risk assessment and audit evidence is planned. — [Journal of Accountancy (Oct 2026)](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2026/oct/ai-and-the-audit-aicpa-publishes-practice-aid-to-assist-auditors/); [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/10/13/aicpa-practice-aid-provides-guidance-to-auditors-on-ai-use-in-financial-statement-audits/191588/); [AICPA](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/news/article/aicpa-releases-guidance-to-help-auditors-address-the-use-of-ai-in-financial-reporting-and-auditing)
- PCAOB: there is no standard specific to AI. Late-2025 staff guidance addresses the reliability of electronic information under AS 1105, and inspectors will focus on AI in 2026. Commentators say an AI-generated exception report, classification, or dashboard alone is not sufficient appropriate audit evidence. — [Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/blog/how-to-use-ai-in-audit-without-creating-inspection-risk/); [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/pcaob-warns-auditors-about-ai-and-pe); [CalCPA](https://www.calcpa.org/whats-happening/articles/ai-standard-setting-regulation-in-audit-part-1)
- Investors and auditors are pressing the PCAOB to accelerate AI guidance. Some firms (e.g., Baker Tilly) prefer guidance over new standards. — [Bloomberg](https://news.bgov.com/financial-accounting/auditors-investors-press-us-watchdog-to-fast-track-ai-guidance)

### Inferences
- The main friction with auditors is evidentiary: an autopilot ledger must produce audit trails (source documents, rule or model rationale, approvals, change logs) that let an auditor test controls and records under AS 1105 / AU-C 500. A SOC 1 report on the system's controls makes auditor reliance much easier.
- The EU AI Act still brings Article 4 AI-literacy and transparency obligations (in force since 2025) and GPAI-provider duties that fall mainly on model vendors. For an EU deployment of accounting automation, GDPR is the binding constraint more than the AI Act.
- If the product expands into credit decisions or lending (e.g., working-capital offers), it would enter high-risk territory under EU Annex III and US state AI laws.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm EU Council final adoption or Official Journal publication of the AI Omnibus, or the final Colorado statute text.
- Did not research UK (HMRC Making Tax Digital, ICAEW AI guidance) or EU e-invoicing mandates.
- Found no PCAOB primary text, only secondary summaries.

## 5. Evidence on AI accuracy in accounting tasks, and the human-in-the-loop debate

### Takeaway
Independent and semi-independent benchmarks consistently show frontier LLM agents are not reliable enough for unreviewed close work. Errors compound month over month (Penrose AccountingBench, 2025). The top model scores about 56% partial credit on month-end tasks, and no model exceeds 2.6% consistency across 8 runs (APEX-Accounting, Mercor/Ramp, Jul 2026). Transaction classification is better (roughly 70 to 87% for general models), and vendor-tuned systems claim about 98%, but those claims come from interested vendors. This evidence is why leading vendors keep humans or CPA firms in the loop.

### Cited Findings
- Penrose AccountingBench (July 2025) ran agents through sequential monthly closes on one year of real SaaS-company data, with a human CPA as baseline. Claude 4 and Grok 4 stayed above 95% for the first months, then degraded. Claude fell below 85% as errors snowballed. Gemini 2.5 Pro, o3, and o4-mini failed to complete a month's close. Penrose's conclusion is that the challenge is moving from doing tasks to doing them correctly. Primary page body could not be fetched, so these numbers come from secondary write-ups. — [Gigazine](https://wbgsv0a.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250724-accountingbench); [Ledge.ai](https://ledge.ai/articles/llm_accounting_benchmark_penrose); [Penrose (primary)](https://accounting.penrose.com/)
- APEX-Accounting (Mercor with Ramp; arXiv 2607.27189, Jul 30, 2026): 160 expert-built month-end tasks (reconciliation, data entry, variance analysis, schedules and accruals) across 10 synthetic US GAAP company "worlds," testing 9 frontier models.
  - Best mean partial credit (Criteria@3) was 56.4%. Best Pass@8 was 21.5%. Pass^8 (correct on all 8 runs) was at most 2.6%.
  - 93 of 160 tasks were never fully solved by any model.
  - 59 to 79% of reviewed failures were reasoning errors (wrong basis, dropped intermediate results), and none were tool-use failures.
  - The authors conclude that reliability, not peak score, is the gap.
  - — [alphaXiv / arXiv 2607.27189](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.27189)
- DualEntry, an AI ERP vendor, benchmarked generalist models on accounting workflows (Mar 2026). The best was 77.3% ("C+"); a secondary summary attributes the top score to "ChatGPT 5.4," with Gemini 3.1 Pro at 66%. Methodology is undisclosed. The vendor said "without system-level controls and validation, errors can quickly cascade through financial reporting." — [Accounting Today (Mar 17, 2026)](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/generalist-ai-model-accuracy-tops-out-at-77-for-accounting)
- Digits, a vendor, published a benchmark on Jun 4, 2026: 2,000 transactions across 4 businesses, scored against GAAP ground truth set by an accountant panel. The best frontier models scored about 81% baseline and about 87% with tools and skills. Outsourced human accountants scored 79.1%. Digits' own Agentic General Ledger scored 97.8% with "zero hallucinations." This is a vendor's own marketing benchmark, it covers classification only, and its chart and text conflict. — [Digits blog](https://digits.com/blog/frontier-models-now-beat-human-accountants/)
- An academic paper, "Evaluating Accounting Reasoning Capabilities of LLMs," was published Jan 2026. It was not fetched. — [arXiv 2601.06707](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06707v1)
- Another study reportedly tested 19 frontier models on 17,792 real transactions from 100+ small businesses and found no general LLM above 70% classification accuracy. It comes from a search-result summary, and the primary source was not identified. Treat it as unverified.
- Human-in-the-loop in the market:
  - Basis frames its model as "accountants and agents working together" and sells to CPA firms. — [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/02/24/basis-raises-100-million-to-deploy-ai-agents-for-accounting-firms/178759/)
  - Digits co-builds with CLA (a top-15 firm) and has an accountant partner program. — [CLA](https://www.claconnect.com/en/media/2026/cla-announcing-initiative-to-cobuild-ainative-accounting-platform)
  - An AI-native-firm founder said "I can't just be an entirely all AI firm." — [Accounting Today podcast](https://www.accountingtoday.com/podcast/the-ai-native-firm)
- Insurers want strong AI governance at CPA firms. — [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/insurers-eyeing-ai-risk-at-cpa-firms)

### Inferences
- The evidence supports high automation on transactional work (categorization, matching) with confidence-gated review, and does not support unreviewed close, accruals, or tax positions. Low run-to-run consistency (Pass^8 at or below 2.6%) argues for deterministic rule layers, ensemble or self-consistency checks, and accounting invariants (trial balance ties, bank-balance tie-outs, subledger-to-GL reconciliation) as hard gates.
- Practical mitigations the evidence points to:
  1. Confidence thresholds with an exception queue. Route low-confidence or high-materiality items to humans, with materiality thresholds per account.
  2. Deterministic validation: every reconciliation must tie to bank or statement balances, with no "plug" entries allowed. Penrose-style failure modes include forcing balances to match.
  3. Immutable audit trails: source doc to entry, model/version, prompt or rule, confidence, and approver, exportable for auditors.
  4. Licensed CPA partner network or affiliated APS CPA firm for tax signing, attest, and periodic review (e.g., quarterly CPA sign-off).
  5. A SOC 1 and SOC 2 Type II, plus a GLBA WISP.
  6. Affirmative AI E&O coverage.
  7. Data portability and wind-down commitments (the Bench and Botkeeper lessons).
  8. Contract terms with a clear allocation of the review duty, marketing that avoids "no review needed" claims, and possibly a capped penalty-reimbursement guarantee as a trust signal.
- "Autopilot" can credibly mean autopilot with exceptions. Full no-human operation for tax filing and year-end close is not yet supported by any independent evidence found.
